title: 'Rate Limits'
description: 'Request quotas and spending caps, the X-RateLimit-* headers, and how to handle 429s.'

Every authenticated route is rate-limited per minute. When you exceed the limit, the request is refused with `429 RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, a `Retry-After` header and the `X-RateLimit-*` headers, so you can back off cleanly.

## Limits set by your key's tier

Your key's tier sets its limits. Read the live values from the `X-RateLimit-*` headers and from the bodies of `409` and `429` refusals rather than hard-coding them.

A tier sets four independent limits:

- **Request rate.** Requests per minute. Exceeding it returns `429 RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
- **Per-trade cap.** The USDC amount of one trade. A larger trade is refused at once with `409 SPENDING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
- **Daily spend cap.** Total USDC bought through the key since 00:00 UTC. This is checked after the trade is accepted: a buy that would exceed it moves to `rejected` and fires a `trade.rejected` webhook. Sells do not count against it.
- **High-value confirmation.** On tiers that have one, a trade at or above the threshold returns `202 Accepted` with `confirmation: { token, expiresAt }`. Release it before `expiresAt` with `POST /v1/trades/{id}/confirm`. This gives your system a chance to stop a runaway bot before it commits a large trade.

There is also a cap on **unfinished trades** per account: a new trade while the account already has its tier's maximum in flight is refused with `429 TRADE_IN_FLIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and a short `Retry-After`.

## Who shares a quota

- The **request rate** is counted per **user**, across every key that user holds, so extra keys do not add throughput. Each key is also counted on its own, with the same limit.
- **Spending caps** are per **key**.
- Read routes and write routes draw from **separate** budgets, each at the tier's per-minute limit:
  - **Read** routes (every `GET`) use a sliding window: smooth, with no bursting.
  - **Write** routes (every rate-limited `POST`) use a token bucket: its capacity equals the limit and it refills continuously, so short bursts are allowed.

The [endpoint reference](/developer-docs/rest-api/endpoint-reference) shows each route's class.

## Response headers

Every rate-limited response, successful or not, carries the state of the budget closest to running out:

```
X-RateLimit-Limit:     100
X-RateLimit-Remaining: 87
X-RateLimit-Reset:     1792000060
```

- `X-RateLimit-Limit`: the per-minute limit.
- `X-RateLimit-Remaining`: requests left before you are refused.
- `X-RateLimit-Reset`: Unix time in **seconds** when the budget is full again.

Pace yourself from these rather than waiting for a `429`.

## Hitting the limit

```http
HTTP/1.1 429 Too Many Requests
Content-Type: application/problem+json
Retry-After: 12
X-RateLimit-Limit: 100
X-RateLimit-Remaining: 0
X-RateLimit-Reset: 1792000072

{
  "type": "https://docs.kash.bot/developer-docs/api-errors/RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED",
  "title": "Rate limit exceeded",
  "status": 429,
  "code": "RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED",
  "detail": "…",
  "instance": "/v1/markets",
  "requestId": "…"
}
```

`Retry-After` is computed for your bucket: the seconds until it admits another request. Always honour it. The TypeScript SDK does this automatically.

If the rate limiter itself is briefly unavailable, requests are refused with `503 RATE_LIMIT_UNAVAILABLE` and a short `Retry-After`. That is not a quota event; retry it.

## Temporarily disabled routes

Separately from rate limiting, an individual route can be temporarily unavailable, returning `503 ROUTE_DISABLED` with a `Retry-After` header. API trade writes can also be temporarily unavailable, returning `503 API_TRADE_PROCESSING_HALTED` with a `Retry-After` header on trade creation and confirmation while reads keep working. Back off and retry in both cases.

## Best practices

- **Read the headers** and keep `X-RateLimit-Remaining` comfortably above zero under steady load.
- **Use conditional requests** for polling. A `304 Not Modified` still counts as a request, but it is cheap. See [ETags](/developer-docs/rest-api/etags).
- **Prefer webhooks to polling** trade status.
- **Cache quotes briefly.** Quote responses carry a short `Cache-Control` max-age.
- **Use the SDK.** It honours `Retry-After` with exponential backoff and jitter, and sends an idempotency key on writes so retries are safe.

## Upgrading your tier

Repeated `RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `SPENDING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` during normal operation means you have outgrown your tier:

- **`free` → `developer`**: email [support@kash.bot](mailto:support@kash.bot) with your account email and a one-line description of your use case.

## Telemetry

`GET /v1/account/usage` (scope `auth:manage`) reports the calling key's rate-limit rejections over the last 24 hours, among other figures:

```bash
curl https://api.kash.bot/v1/account/usage \
  -H "X-API-Key: $KASH_API_KEY" | jq '.data.auth["24h"].rateLimitRejections'
```
