**HTTP status:** 429 · **Title:** "Too many trades in flight" · **Retry-After:** computed per response

## When it fires

`POST /v1/trades` (or `POST /v1/trades/{id}/confirm` for a high-value trade) when your account already has the
maximum number of trades **in flight** for your key's tier. A trade is in flight from the moment it is accepted
until it settles (`completed`, `failed` or `rejected`). Trades placed through the Kash app count too: the limit
is per account, not per key.

**Nothing was placed.** The request was refused before anything was written, so retrying it, with the same
`Idempotency-Key` or `clientRequestId`, is a fresh attempt and never a duplicate.

## Why the limit exists

Your account's trades execute one after another, in the order you placed them. A queue deeper than Kash
can work through in time would not make your trades faster; the tail would expire unexecuted. Refusing at the door
tells you immediately and lets you decide what to resend.

Your key's tier sets the maximum; the response body's `maxInFlight` gives it. A trade stops counting once it settles, or after a timeout if it has not settled.

This is separate from [`RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`](/developer-docs/api-errors/RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED): that limits how fast requests
**arrive**; this limits how many trades are **unfinished**.

## Response body

In addition to the standard fields, the problem body carries two top-level members:

- `inFlight`: how many of your trades were in flight when you were refused
- `maxInFlight`: your limit

## How to fix

- **Honour `Retry-After`.** The TypeScript SDK retries 429 responses automatically and honours `Retry-After`.
- **Track your trades to completion** ([webhooks](/developer-docs/rest-api/webhooks/overview) or `GET /v1/trades/{id}`) and keep fewer than your limit
  outstanding, instead of firing and forgetting.
